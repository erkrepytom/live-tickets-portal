Getting there

  • Underground to Wembley Park (Jubilee or Metropolitan line), then walk up Olympic Way.
  • Chiltern Railways from Marylebone to Wembley Stadium station, next to the ground.

Seating tips

  • Side sections give the best view of the whole pitch.
  • Upper tiers are usually the cheapest.
  • For a group, buy all seats in one order so you sit together.

What to bring and what gets refused

  • Bring your passport or a photo ID. Resale tickets sometimes carry another person's name, and the partner explains by email what to show.
  • Backpacks and large bags are often refused at big stadiums. Carry only the essentials in a small bag.
  • Bottles, flares and sharp objects are banned. Security checks can be slow at big matches.

Best section for each fan

  • Families. Side stands and middle tiers: good view, calmer atmosphere and easier exit.
  • Loud support. Behind the goals is where the ultras stand. It is the loudest area, and almost everyone stands.
  • Your team's end. The partner separates home and away sections. Pick your team's side and avoid wearing the rival shirt in the wrong section.

Is buying resale safe? How do I avoid scams?

Buy only on an identified platform with published terms. On Ticketmaster, payment and delivery happen inside your account, and resale tickets are labelled as such. Be wary of bank transfers or payment apps to a private person, prices far below the market, and screenshots of a ticket as 'proof': the same QR code can be sold many times.

Guide: scam warning signs →

Will my card work? Are there extra fees?

Your bank's fraud checks sometimes block foreign purchases. Tell your bank before you buy and keep a second card handy. If the price is in a foreign currency, your card may add a foreign transaction fee and use its own exchange rate.

Guide: final price, currency and fees →

Are tickets in my name? Do I need ID?

Some clubs and competitions issue tickets in the buyer's name and check ID at the gate. Check the rules on the event page before you buy and bring photo ID.

Guide: named tickets and ID →

Is the ticket digital?

For most events, yes: the ticket sits in the Ticketmaster app or account. When ticket transfer is not available, everyone on the order must enter together, and screenshots or PDFs are not accepted.

Ticketmaster Help Center →

Can I buy as many tickets as I want?

No. Many events have a ticket limit per person, per card or per household, shown under 'More Info' on the event page. Going over the limit can get the order cancelled.

See prices and sections at Ticketmaster →

What if the event is cancelled or postponed?

If it is cancelled, Ticketmaster refunds the original payment method with no action needed from you. If it is postponed or rescheduled, your ticket stays valid; refunds depend on the organizer.

Read Ticketmaster's purchase policy →

Why is the price above face value?

Resale tickets follow demand and can cost more than face value. On Ticketmaster's seat map you can see whether a seat is a first sale or resale before you pay.

See prices and sections at Ticketmaster →

Can I cancel my order?

Usually not: under Ticketmaster's policy all sales are final, and refunds only exist in the cases the organizer allows. Check the event, date and seats before you pay.
