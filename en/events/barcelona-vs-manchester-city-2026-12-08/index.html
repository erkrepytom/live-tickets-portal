Getting there

  • Metro L3 to Les Corts or Palau Reial, or L5 to Collblanc or Badal.
  • Champions League games are on weeknights. Book a hotel near a metro line.

Seating tips

  • Side sections give the best view of the whole pitch.
  • Upper tiers are usually the cheapest.
  • For a group, buy all seats in one order so you sit together.

What to bring and what gets refused

  • Bring your passport or a photo ID. Resale tickets sometimes carry another person's name: check with the seller what to show.
  • Backpacks and large bags are often refused at European stadiums. Carry only the essentials in a small bag.
  • Bottles, flares and sharp objects are banned. Security checks can be slow at big matches.

Best section for each fan

  • Families. Side stands and middle tiers: good view, calmer atmosphere and easier exit.
  • Loud support. Behind the goals is where the ultras stand. It is the loudest area, and almost everyone stands.
  • Your team's end. Stadiums separate home and away sections. Pick your team's side and avoid wearing the rival shirt in the wrong section.

Before you buy

Barça tickets are issued by name and ID is checked at the gate. In the Champions League, UEFA rules limit away fans from buying home-section seats, and a card from the away team's country may cause problems.
