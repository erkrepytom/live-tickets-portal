Getting there

  • Metro L3 to Les Corts or Palau Reial, or L5 to Collblanc or Badal.
  • Arrive 90 minutes early for El Clásico. Security lines are slow.

Seating tips

  • Side sections give the best view of the whole pitch.
  • Upper tiers are usually the cheapest.
  • For a group, buy all seats in one order so you sit together.

What to bring and what gets refused

  • Bring your passport or a photo ID. Resale tickets sometimes carry another person's name: check with the seller what to show.
  • Backpacks and large bags are often refused at European stadiums. Carry only the essentials in a small bag.
  • Bottles, flares and sharp objects are banned. Security checks can be slow at big matches.

Best section for each fan

  • Families. Side stands and middle tiers: good view, calmer atmosphere and easier exit.
  • Loud support. Behind the goals is where the ultras stand. It is the loudest area, and almost everyone stands.
  • Your team's end. Stadiums separate home and away sections. Pick your team's side and avoid wearing the rival shirt in the wrong section.

Before you buy

Barça tickets are issued by name: the club asks for each fan's details a few days before and checks ID at the gate. For El Clásico, the official sale is short and favours members.

Is buying resale safe? How do I avoid scams?

Buy only from an identified company with published terms and a written guarantee. Be wary of bank transfers or payment apps to a private person, prices far below the market, and screenshots of tickets as proof: the same QR code can be sold many times.

Can my card be declined?

It can: bank fraud checks sometimes block foreign purchases. Tell your bank in advance and carry a second card. Prices are in a foreign currency and your bank may add a fee.

Are tickets in my name? Do I need ID?

Some clubs and competitions issue tickets in the buyer's name and check ID at the gate. Bring your passport and check the event's rules before you buy.
